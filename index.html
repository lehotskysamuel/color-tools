OKLCh Atlas

Every color an sRGB screen can show, placed in OKLab so that distance on the page matches how different two colors look. One unit is the same perceptual step on every axis of every view. Pick a paint to see where it sits, and compare the paints' hull with Pointer's gamut of real surface colors.

Paints

Click a paint to show or hide it as a dot in the views. Showing a paint also picks it, so double-click a shown paint to pick it. The slices show paints within ΔEOK 0.04 of their plane, fainter the farther away. The solid draws every dot on top; orbit it to judge depth.

Every paint is shown in the color its maker shows on its website, so all ranges are compared on the same kind of data: screen colors, clipped to sRGB. For Game Color and Model Color that is Vallejo's printed chart converted for display, for Squidmar Color a sample of its marketing images, for Kimera Kolors the shop's image. The data also keeps the charts' print colors and measurements of Kimera Kolors from artistpigments.org (CC BY-NC 4.0), which reach less far.

ΔEOK of about 0.02 is one just-noticeable difference. Hatched areas are outside sRGB: no screen color exists there. Every color sits on the same middle gray on purpose, in both themes, because a tinted, bright or dark surround changes how the colors look.

Color math: OKLab by Björn Ottosson (2020). Straight-line OKLab distance is a good model for small and medium differences and overstates very large ones.

Paint sets side by side. Each shows its paints and their hull inside Pointer's gamut of real surface colors, the way the Atlas first draws them, with the share of Pointer's gamut the hull covers and how many paints of each type the set has. Pick each column's set from the same layouts as the Atlas. Orbit or zoom any render and the others follow.

Every render is the Atlas's gamut solid as it starts: Pointer's gamut as a wireframe, the hull of the set's paints as a solid, every paint as a dot, with the labeled axes. Drag to orbit and scroll to zoom any of them. Hovering a color marks it in all of them, so you can see where a paint of one set lies against the hull of another.

The coverage is the share of Pointer's gamut's volume in OKLab that lies inside the hull of the set's paints; the mark on each bar is sRGB's own share. A hull measures spread, not which colors the paints can mix. The Game Color and Model Color sets come from Vallejo's printed charts and Kimera Kolors from measurements of dried paint. Squidmar Color is sampled from marketing images made for screens, so its figures are not directly comparable with the others'. A paint set counts only the paints the catalog has a color for; the rest, such as varnishes, are listed as having no color data.
